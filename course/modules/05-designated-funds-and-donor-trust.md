# Module 5: Designated Funds and Donor Trust

## The Most Sacred Obligation in Nonprofit Finance

When a donor gives to a specific cause — a building project, a mission trip, a scholarship fund, a children's programme — they are not making a general contribution. They are placing a specific trust in you.

> **Designated giving must be used for its stated purpose. This is not just an ethical standard — it is a legal obligation in most jurisdictions.**

Failing to honour donor designations is one of the most serious failures a mission organization can commit. It damages trust, exposes leadership to legal liability, and can permanently destroy the organization's reputation.

Fund accounting — the practice of tracking designated money separately — is the accounting discipline built to prevent this.

---

## Fund Accounting

**Fund accounting** is the system used by nonprofits, churches, and government entities to account for money that is restricted to a specific purpose.

Unlike commercial accounting, where the primary goal is measuring profit, fund accounting's primary goal is demonstrating that money was used as intended.

### Types of Funds

**Unrestricted funds** — money the organization can use for any purpose consistent with its mission.
- General operating income (undesignated tithes, offerings)
- Fundraising proceeds without specific restrictions

**Temporarily restricted funds** — money that must be used for a specific purpose or within a specific time period.
- Building fund donations
- Mission trip contributions
- Project-specific grants
- Annual campaign gifts

**Permanently restricted funds** — money that must be held indefinitely; only the income it generates may be spent.
- Endowments
- Permanent memorials

The standard for fund accounting is set by bodies like the **International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) for SMEs**, and for US organizations, the **Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) ASC 958** for nonprofits. The principle is universal: track restricted money separately.

---

## Practical Fund Tracking

In practice, fund accounting means:

1. **Tag each inflow** with its fund designation at the point of receipt
2. **Track the balance** of each fund separately — not just the total bank balance
3. **Record each outflow** against the fund it was drawn from
4. **Report on each fund** — showing beginning balance, inflows, outflows, and ending balance
5. **Never use restricted funds for unrestricted purposes** without donor consent and proper documentation

### Example: Building Fund

| Date | Description | In | Out | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 | Opening balance | | | ₦0 |
| Jan 15 | Member donation — Building | ₦500,000 | | ₦500,000 |
| Feb 3 | Member donation — Building | ₦200,000 | | ₦700,000 |
| Mar 10 | Architect fees — Building phase 1 | | ₦250,000 | ₦450,000 |
| Mar 31 | Closing balance | | | **₦450,000** |

The organization may have ₦2,000,000 total in the bank — but only ₦450,000 of that is available for building purposes. The rest belongs to other funds.

## Budget Allocations

A **budget** is a financial plan — a commitment made in advance about how money will be received and spent over a given period.

### Why Budgets Matter

Budgets serve multiple functions:
- **Planning** — forces leadership to think ahead about income and expenses
- **Control** — provides a target against which actual spending is measured
- **Accountability** — makes overspending visible and subject to review
- **Communication** — tells the congregation or board what the organization intends to do

### Budget vs. Actual Reporting

One of the most powerful reports in nonprofit finance is the **Budget vs. Actual** report. For each category, it shows:
- What was budgeted
- What actually happened
- The variance (difference)
- The variance as a percentage

This report answers the question: *"Are we on track?"*

| Category | Budget | Actual | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tithes & Offerings | ₦1,200,000 | ₦1,050,000 | −₦150,000 (−12.5%) |
| Staff Salaries | ₦600,000 | ₦600,000 | ₦0 |
| Building Maintenance | ₦100,000 | ₦143,000 | −₦43,000 (−43%) |
| Outreach Ministry | ₦150,000 | ₦88,000 | +₦62,000 (+41%) |

Variances are not automatically bad — but they require explanation and leadership attention.

## Fund Transfers

Sometimes money needs to move between funds or between bank accounts. A **fund transfer** is not an income or expense — it is a movement of existing assets.

**Example:** Moving ₦200,000 from the main account to the building savings account.
- The organization's total assets do not change
- But the location and designation of the money has changed

Transfers must be recorded explicitly — not buried as unexplained debits and credits in your records.
