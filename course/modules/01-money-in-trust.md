# Module 1: Money in Trust

## The First Principle of Financial Stewardship

Before we talk about spreadsheets, software, or even numbers — we need to talk about what money in an organization actually *is*.

When someone gives to a church, a ministry, or an NGO, they are not making a purchase. They are placing their trust in you. The money they give is not yours — it is held in trust, on behalf of a mission, on behalf of a community, often on behalf of God himself.

This is called **stewardship** — and it changes everything about how money should be handled.

---

## The Stewardship Principle

In accounting, the foundational idea is this:

> **An organization exists separately from the people who run it.**

This is called the **Entity Concept** — one of the most important principles in accounting. The organization's money is not the treasurer's money. It is not the pastor's money. It is not the board's money. It belongs to the entity, held for its stated mission.

This has practical consequences:
- Personal and organizational finances must never be mixed
- Every financial decision must be traceable back to the organization's purpose
- Records must be kept as if anyone could ask to see them — because they can

---

## Why Records Are Not Optional

Many mission-driven organizations start with informal systems: a notebook, a shared spreadsheet, a memory. This works — until it doesn't.

Without proper records:
- You cannot tell donors how their gifts were used
- You cannot spot errors, fraud, or misuse
- You cannot plan a budget against real numbers
- You cannot produce a report for your board
- You cannot survive a financial review or audit

The absence of records is not just an inconvenience — it is a failure of stewardship. It breaks trust.

---

## The Accounting Equation

Every financial system in the world — from the simplest church ledger to the most complex corporate ERP — rests on one equation:

```
Assets = Liabilities + Equity (Net Assets)
```

In plain English:
- **Assets** — everything the organization owns or is owed (cash, bank balances, equipment)
- **Liabilities** — everything the organization owes to others (debts, unpaid bills)
- **Equity / Net Assets** — what's left after obligations are met; for non-profits this is called *net assets* or *fund balance*

This equation must always balance. If it doesn't, something is wrong.

---

## What Good Financial Stewardship Looks Like

An organization with healthy financial stewardship can answer these questions at any time:

1. How much money do we have, and where is it?
2. Where did the money come from?
3. Where did the money go?
4. Are we staying within our budget?
5. Can we account for every designated gift?
6. Who approved each transaction?

If any of these questions takes more than a few minutes to answer, the financial system needs work.
