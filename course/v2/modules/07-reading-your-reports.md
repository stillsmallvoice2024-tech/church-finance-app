# Module 7: Reading Your Reports

Records answer the question "what happened?" Reports answer the question "what does it mean?"

If you have done everything in the previous modules — recording inflows and outflows, categorizing transactions, tracking funds, reconciling banks — then your data is ready to speak. This module teaches you to read what it says.

| Report | What it answers |

## The Income and Expenditure Statement

This is the most important report for most mission organizations. It is sometimes called:
- The Income Statement
- The Statement of Activities (in nonprofit accounting)
- The Profit and Loss (in commercial accounting — "profit" becomes "surplus" for nonprofits)

**What it shows:** All income and all expenses over a chosen period (a month, a quarter, a year), organized by category, with a total at the bottom.

**How to read it:**

```
INCOME
  Sunday Tithes               ₦1,050,000
  Building Fund Donations       ₦700,000
  Grant — Children's Programme  ₦500,000
  ─────────────────────────────────────
  Total Income                ₦2,250,000

EXPENSES
  Pastoral Salaries             ₦600,000
  Facilities                    ₦143,000
  Youth Ministry                 ₦88,000
  Children's Programme          ₦490,000
  Administration                 ₦45,000
  ─────────────────────────────────────
  Total Expenses              ₦1,366,000

NET SURPLUS                     ₦884,000
```

**Net surplus** means income exceeded expenses — the organization is operating within its means. A **net deficit** means the opposite, and requires attention.

**Questions to ask when reading this report:**
- Is total income growing, stable, or declining compared to previous periods?
- Which expense categories are increasing? Is that increase justified?
- Is the surplus or deficit consistent with what we expected?
- Are any income categories missing that we expected to see?

## Cash Position: What You Actually Have

The Income & Expenditure statement shows flows over time. But sometimes the most urgent question is simpler: **what do we have right now?**

The answer is your **cash position** — the sum of all bank account balances at this moment.

This matters because:
- A surplus on the Income & Expenditure statement does not mean the cash is available — it might be restricted to a designated fund
- An organization can show a healthy surplus and still run out of cash if income arrives late and expenses are due now

**Cash runway** is a practical indicator: take your total unrestricted cash and divide by your average monthly operating expenses. The result is the number of months you can keep operating if no new income arrives.

```
Cash Runway = Total Unrestricted Cash ÷ Monthly Operating Expenses
```

A healthy minimum is 3 months. Less than 1 month is a warning sign that requires immediate action.

## The Budget vs. Actual Report

Covered in detail in Module 6. In practice, this report should be:
- Reviewed monthly by the finance team
- Reviewed quarterly by the board
- Available to any ministry leader for their own budget line

When a significant variance appears (more than 10% difference from budget), it should trigger a brief explanation: *"Youth Ministry is under-budget because the annual conference was postponed to next quarter."* Documented explanations prevent confusion and demonstrate active financial management.

## What Clariva Does Not Yet Produce: The Balance Sheet

The balance sheet — also called the **Statement of Financial Position** — is a snapshot of everything the organization owns and owes on a specific date.

```
ASSETS                              LIABILITIES
Cash & Bank Accounts  ₦1,675,000   Loan — Building     ₦500,000
Equipment              ₦250,000    Accounts Payable      ₦80,000
                                   ────────────────────────────
                                   Total Liabilities    ₦580,000

                                   NET ASSETS
                                   Unrestricted       ₦1,095,000
                                   Restricted           ₦250,000
                                   ────────────────────────────
                                   Total Net Assets   ₦1,345,000

Total Assets         ₦1,925,000   Total Liabilities + Net Assets  ₦1,925,000
```

The balance sheet requires tracking **three things together**: what you hold, what you owe, and the difference. Clariva currently tracks what you hold (bank balances, FX) and the activity that affects your fund balances — but it does not track liabilities (loans, accounts payable) or fixed assets (equipment values).

**What this means for you:**

If your organization is small and has no loans or significant fixed assets, Clariva's reports give you everything you need. Your cash position and category balances tell the complete practical story.

If your organization is larger, carries loans, owns significant assets (vehicles, buildings, equipment), or is required to produce formal audited accounts, you will need to work with an accountant who maintains a full balance sheet. Clariva's data is the transaction foundation that accountant will work from — your inflows, outflows, and bank records are the primary evidence for everything else.

Many mission organizations start with Clariva as their primary system and bring in an accountant once or twice a year to produce formal accounts using Clariva's data. This is a practical and cost-effective approach.

## The Disclosure Principle

Beyond producing accurate numbers, good financial reporting means providing enough context for the reader to understand what they are seeing.

If a large unusual transaction appears in your reports — a one-time grant, an emergency repair, a refund of a prior payment — note it. A brief explanation prevents misunderstanding and demonstrates active stewardship.

Items worth disclosing:
- Significant restricted funds and what they are designated for
- Any loans or obligations not visible in day-to-day transaction records
- Unusual or non-recurring transactions that affect comparability
- Transactions involving leadership or their family members (conflicts of interest)

Transparency is not a vulnerability. It is the mark of an organization that has nothing to hide — and that confidence is the most valuable thing you can offer your donors.
